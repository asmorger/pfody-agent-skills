The Identity Seam

Identity is the hardest thing to slice, because every workflow depends on it and none of it is a workflow. This is one survey and one brief for user management, and the trick that makes the brief shippable while the biggest question is still open.

The artifacts themselves are in the repo at docs/examples/. This page is the reasoning around them: why the slice is drawn where it is, and the full question set the survey produced, sorted by who can actually answer each one.

Start with the question you can answer without a meeting

“We are uncertain what security group controls and whether we still need it” sounds like a question for the business. Most of it isn't. The old system already contains the answer, and reading it out took one exhaustive search.

groups defined

41

Rows in USER_GROUP, accumulated over twelve years.

referenced in code

9

Distinct group names appearing as string literals, across 31 call sites. That is the entire authorization model.

referenced nowhere

31

Not in client code, reports, or stored procedures. Held by 142 people who gain nothing from them.

in no group at all

87

Sign in fine and can read everything in their org, because groups are only checked on writes.

So your assumption was half right. It is role-shaped, but there is no role: a “security group” is a name that 31 scattered IsInGroup("WHSE_SUPER") comparisons test against. Nothing maps a group to a capability. The mapping is the 31 call sites, which is why nobody can tell you what a group controls, and why the answer was never going to arrive from a conversation.

What remains genuinely a business question is much narrower: can the 31 unreferenced groups be retired, and what do the 9 live ones mean in warehouse terms. The first is a yes/no with evidence attached. The second needs twenty minutes with a specific long-tenured supervisor, not your PM.

Three things that make this area different

  • You cannot slice it by workflow, so slice it by population. Every screen depends on sign-in, so there is no vertical slice of “a bit of authentication.” There is, however, a perfectly good slice of “six people authenticate differently.” Smallest and most tolerant population first, by explicit allow-list, with the old path still hot.
  • Split authentication from authorization, and move only the first. Entra answers who is this. USER_GROUP keeps answering what may they do. This is the seam, and it is what lets the slice ship while the security group question stays open for another three slices.
  • Do not make this your first area. The practice says no schema migration and no shared-code refactor inside a slice, and identity wants both. Get two self-contained workflows shipped first so the team has calibrated its appetite and the comparison harness exists. This sample is numbered 012 on purpose.

The question set, sorted by who can answer it

This is the part worth stealing. The survey produced twenty questions, and sending all twenty to one PM is how a markup session turns back into a requirements meeting. They sort into three piles, and only the first pile goes on the markup sheet. Flagged items are the ones teams usually discover too late.

For the business · 13

Goes on the markup sheet. Each is a ruling, not research.

Q01A soft-deleted account can still sign in. It has never happened in 24 months. Fix it, or is someone relying on it?

The log count is what makes this a thirty-second answer instead of a risk assessment.

Q0231 of 41 groups are referenced nowhere in the software. Do they mean anything to you outside it, or can they be retired?

Most of your security-group uncertainty resolves here, with evidence rather than opinion.

Q0423 people belong to more than one organization. Real need, or years of data entry?

Changes the tenancy model. If it is data entry, the new model gets dramatically simpler.

Q05Three reports show data across all of a user's organizations regardless of the one they picked at sign-in. Correct, or a leak?

If it is a leak it is a live one, today, and it stops being a modernization question.

Q06Four warehouse stations appear to share one login. Does every worker need their own identity now, or do station accounts stay? easy to miss

Per-user licensing, per-person audit trails, and shop-floor workflow all turn on this. It is also the question most likely to be answered by the warehouse rather than by product.

Q07Entra makes the work email the sign-in identity. Legacy usernames are printed on pick tickets and appear in 24 months of audit rows. Must the old username survive as a visible field? easy to miss

Cheap to answer now, expensive to retrofit after the identity model is built.

Q09Are the third-party users people, or systems? easy to miss

If any are systems, they need API credentials rather than user identities and they leave the identity project entirely. Twelve accounts in this survey already look like integrations wearing user accounts.

Q10No password can be migrated, so all 412 accounts re-enroll. Who owns that cutover and the communication to external customers? easy to miss

Unsalted SHA-1 cannot be handed to any identity provider. This is an unavoidable consequence of a technical finding, and it lands as a change-management cost, not an engineering task.

Q11Do customer administrators need to manage their own users? easy to miss

Delegated administration constrains the external identity provider more than any technical consideration will. Answer it before the “unknown system” is chosen, not after.

Q13External identity is licensed per monthly active user. Has that recurring cost been priced against the directive? easy to miss

A directive issued before anyone counted external users is worth re-examining while it is still cheap to change.

Q03 Q08 Q1287 people in no group with full read access; what an external user must never see, as policy rather than current behavior; and whether ADMIN granting ADMIN unaudited is acceptable to carry forward.

Three rulings that each turn a discovered accident into a deliberate choice.

For a named specialist · 3

Not for the PM. Each needs one particular person for twenty minutes, and getting that time is worth more than a week of guessing.

S0134 tables name a user as the actor on a row. Is there a regulatory requirement that those always resolve to a named person?

Controller or compliance. Determines whether a user record can ever be retired, which in turn determines whether the new system needs its own permanent user ledger.

S02What does each of the 9 live group names actually gate, in business terms?

The longest-tenured warehouse supervisor. We can read the 31 call sites; we cannot tell you which of them matter, and the PM cannot either.

S03Which of the 12 integration accounts belong to systems still in use, and where are their credentials stored?

Integrations owner. These break the moment internal sign-in moves, so this answer gates a later slice.

Still ours to verify · 4

Not questions for anyone. Work we owe before the brief is shapeable, listed so it is not mistaken for a blocker on someone else.

V01Does the client cache the resolved permission set after sign-in?

Determines whether a group change takes effect immediately or at next login, which the new app has to match.

V02Does any stored procedure or report read USER_GROUP directly?

If so, 31 call sites is an undercount and the headline finding above needs revising. This is exactly the kind of thing that gets regenerated rather than argued about.

V03 V04Whether the three cross-org reports are reachable by external users, and whether anything other than the desktop client calls usp_AuthenticateUser.

Both bound the blast radius. V04 in particular decides whether the slice can be as small as it claims.

## What this slice deliberately does not decide

What "security group" should become. Entra supplies identity only. The
permission set still resolves from USER_GROUP through the same 31 call sites.

This is the seam that lets the slice ship: the open question from the survey
(Q02, S02) does not need an answer for another three slices, because nothing
on the authorization side of the seam changes.

## Decisions outstanding: 0

The Decisions outstanding: 0 line is load-bearing. Eight of the twenty survey questions are unanswered when this brief is frozen, and the brief still reads zero, because every one of them became a DEFER row with a reason instead of a dependency. That is the difference between deferring and stalling.

What the rest of the ladder looks like

Each later slice is gated by a specific answered question rather than by a date. Writing it this way makes the dependency visible to the people who owe the answers.

Two things this ordering buys you. The integration accounts get discovered and fixed before the slice that would have broken them, which is the kind of outage that otherwise happens at 6am on a Monday. And the choice of external identity provider, currently a question mark in the directive, gets made last, by which point you will know how many external users there are, whether any are systems, and whether customers need to administer their own. That is a far better position to choose from than today.
